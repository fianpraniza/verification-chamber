# Image similarity for "same scene, same camera pose" on low-texture scenes (white styrofoam floor under water, torch-lit, iOS with Apple frameworks only)

Scope note: research done 2026-11-09. Apple doc facts come from the developer.apple.com DocC JSON endpoints (same content as the HTML pages). Points marked **[own derivation]** or **[own simulation]** are this researcher's reasoning or a quick numpy check, not a cited source. Items marked **[3rd-party]** need a non-Apple library.

## Q1. Why NCC and template matching become meaningless when image variance is low (with any rule of thumb, and the max-over-shifts bias)

### Takeaway
NCC divides by the patch standard deviation. When the stable scene has almost no variance, the score is controlled by whatever does vary: sensor noise, glints and ripples. Classical photogrammetry and tracking work says correlation "inherently needs" texture, and gates matches with a local-texture test (local std, or the smaller eigenvalue of the gradient structure tensor). The measured 0.22 between two frames of a still phone shows that roughly 3/4 of the thumbnail's variance changed in 4 s. It does not show that the pose changed.

### Cited Findings
- A 1995 ISPRS workshop paper on correlation matching says correlation "inherently needs a spatial greylevel variance (texture)", and that low-texture regions give wrong correlation results. It used the mean standard deviation in a 7×7 neighbourhood as a texture measure. Correlation of truly corresponding regions reached its maximum level at a local std of about 8 grey levels (8-bit), which the authors call "sufficient". The accept/reject threshold was set where the histograms of correct and wrong matches intersect, so it was chosen from data, not fixed in advance. — [IAPRS Vol. 30-5W1, "From Pixels to Sequences", Zurich 1995, p.133](https://goobi.tib.eu/viewer/!fulltext/1067806741/133)
- Shi and Tomasi, "Good Features to Track" (CVPR 1994), define a window as trackable when both eigenvalues of its 2×2 gradient (structure) matrix are large. In practice min(λ1, λ2) > λ, where λ sits above the noise level and the matrix is well conditioned. This is the standard "matchability" test for patch matching. — [Shi & Tomasi 1994 (PDF)](https://users.cs.duke.edu/~tomasi/papers/shi/shiCvpr94.pdf); [CMU record](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/good-features-to-track); lecture summary of the two conditions: [UCF CAP6411 Lecture 17](https://www.cs.ucf.edu/courses/cap6411/cap6411/spring2006/Lecture17.pdf)
- Szeliski's textbook recommends looking for maxima of the smaller eigenvalue to find good features to track. It also treats NCC in the translational-alignment chapter: Section 8.1 in the 1st edition, 9.1 in the 2nd. — [Szeliski, Computer Vision: Algorithms and Applications (book site)](https://szeliski.org/Book/); the Penn State CSE486 slides cite Szeliski §8.1 for normalized correlation: [PSU lecture 7](https://www.cse.psu.edu/~rtc12/CSE486/lecture07.pdf). (I could not extract the book PDF to quote the exact sentence about NCC being undefined for zero-variance patches. See Gaps.)
- Implementations treat NCC as undefined where the patch under the template has zero variance, and force the score to 0 or to a fallback cost for near-zero denominators. — [ncc.py (IPFS mirror)](https://ipfs.desci.com/ipfs/bafybeick7ibz6v5mpvdk36hq7q4i266xjjoclhe6ojj4a5wcw2z3z3wqsi/ncc.py)
- Remote-sensing patent text notes that flat, texture-less water makes NCC confuse matches and produce false positives. — [US 8594375 "Advanced cloud cover assessment"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8594375)
- With positively autocorrelated (for example, blurred) images, the effective number of independent samples in a correlation coefficient falls below the pixel count. That inflates the spread of chance correlations and the false-positive rate. — [Clifford, Richardson & Hémon 1989, Biometrics 45:123–134](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_crossref_primary_10_2307_2532039); follow-up [Dutilleul 1993, Biometrics 49](https://scilit.com/publications/dacf8510d9cb6a08f04421a61bccbb9d)

### Inferences
- **[own derivation]** Model each frame as stable scene S plus time-varying part N: sensor noise, torch glints and ripple caustics, all independent between frames and of equal variance. Then the expected same-pose NCC = var(S) / (var(S) + var(N)) = SNR/(1+SNR). This is the classical attenuation-of-correlation result. **[own simulation]** On 64×48 thumbnails this held to 3 decimals: SNR 0.25 → 0.20, SNR 0.5 → 0.34, SNR 1 → 0.50.
- **[own derivation]** Applying that model, the measured 0.22 at zero motion means var(S)/var(N) ≈ 0.28. About 78% of the thumbnail's variance changed in 4 s, so the reference frame is mostly a snapshot of glints and ripples. Any threshold on this NCC is a threshold on water state, not on pose. Box changes or height changes alter only the small stable part, so they move NCC by an amount comparable to the frame-to-frame jitter.
- **[own simulation]** Max-over-shifts bias: for two independent noise images (64×48, 3×3 box blur), zero-shift NCC had mean 0.003 and sd 0.038. The max over the ±3 px window (49 shifts) had mean 0.068 and 99th percentile 0.125. The shift search alone adds about +0.07 to +0.12 of free positive score on pure noise. On this scene it eats a large share of the 0.22. A gate threshold of "NCC > 0.15" would barely beat chance.
- **Rule of thumb to carry forward:** before trusting NCC, compute a texture or matchability measure on the reference: global std, mean local std in 7×7, or min-eigenvalue of the structure tensor (vImage/Accelerate can do the convolutions). Refuse the NCC gate when that measure falls below a calibrated floor. The 1995 figure of local std ≈ 8 grey levels is one data point, not a universal constant. Calibrate the floor by checking that repeat frames of a still phone give NCC well above the noise-plus-shift-search baseline.
- Glints are bright, sparse, high-amplitude outliers, and NCC is a least-squares statistic, so a few glint pixels dominate it. Robust options: clip highlights (for example at the 95th percentile), take a temporal median of several frames, or rank-transform. These reduce the problem but cannot create stable texture that the scene does not have.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the exact Szeliski sentence on zero-variance NCC (PDF not extractable here). The section reference (1st ed. §8.1 / 2nd ed. §9.1) comes from the PSU slides and memory. Verify wording before quoting.
- I found no published universal threshold for "minimum texture for NCC". The literature sets thresholds per dataset (histogram intersection, or a fraction of the max eigenvalue as in OpenCV's qualityLevel).

## Q2. Apple Vision VNGenerateImageFeaturePrintRequest: purpose, distance ranges, sensitivity to scale/viewpoint vs content

### Takeaway
Feature prints are upper-layer embeddings from a classification network, built for *semantic* similarity, meaning "same kind of thing". They are not built for geometric or pose identity. Apple publishes no distance thresholds. Distances are revision-specific and can't be compared across revisions. On a scene that is "white floor plus water" in every box, expect feature print distance to separate neither (a) a different identical-looking box nor (b) a modest height change reliably.

### Cited Findings
- API: "An image-based request to generate feature prints from an image." It returns VNFeaturePrintObservation and is available from iOS 13. `computeDistance(_:to:)`: "Shorter distances indicate greater similarity between feature prints." The Swift-native equivalents are `GenerateImageFeaturePrintRequest` / `FeaturePrintObservation.distance(to:)` (iOS 18). — [VNGenerateImageFeaturePrintRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vngenerateimagefeatureprintrequest); [computeDistance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnfeatureprintobservation/computedistance(_:to:)); [GenerateImageFeaturePrintRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/generateimagefeatureprintrequest); [FeaturePrintObservation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/featureprintobservation)
- Revisions: Revision1 (iOS 13) and Revision2 (iOS 17). By default an app uses the latest revision of the SDK it was compiled against. — [Revision1](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vngenerateimagefeatureprintrequestrevision1); [Revision2](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vngenerateimagefeatureprintrequestrevision2)
- Comparing prints from different revisions fails with `Code=12 "The revision of the observations do not match"`. An Apple engineer's advice: pin the revision, or reprocess stored prints. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 732876](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/732876)
- WWDC19 session 222 says the feature print comes from the upper layers of a classification network. Those layers keep "the salient information necessary to perform classification while discarding any redundant or unnecessary information". Smaller distance = "more similar ... in a semantic sense". In Apple's demo, two visually dissimilar cat photos were closer than visually similar pairs of different animals. Image search returned "other images of people in coffee shop and restaurant settings". — [WWDC19 Session 222, Understanding Images in Vision Framework](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/222/); notes: [wwdcnotes](https://wwdcnotes.com/documentation/wwdc19-222-understanding-images-in-vision-framework/)
- The meaning of individual elements is undocumented, so `computeDistance` is the only practical interface. — [shu223, zenn.dev](https://zenn.dev/shu223/articles/vision_image-feature-print)
- Reported ranges (developer anecdotes, not Apple):
  - One open-source photo-clustering project says Revision 1 distances range 0.0–40.0 and uses 10.0 as a "similar" threshold (≈ ¼ of max). — [verny-tran/PhotoClustering (mirror)](https://gh.r-mj.com/verny-tran/PhotoClustering)
  - A forum post reports that identical image files did not give distance 0. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 721760](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/721760)
  - A Medium write-up describes computeDistance as plain Euclidean distance between the vectors. — [MWM.io on Medium](https://medium.com/@MWM.io/apples-vision-framework-exploring-advanced-image-similarity-techniques-f7bb7d008763) (403 when fetched; claim taken from search snippet only)

### Inferences
- Because the descriptor is trained to drop "unnecessary" detail and keep category-level content, it is built to be fairly tolerant of exactly the changes the gate must catch: a modest scale change, a slightly different viewpoint, and the same kind of white box. Two different white styrofoam boxes with water are, semantically, the same image.
- Its likely use here is coarse sanity: catching that the camera points at something else (a hand, a table, the room) or that the frame is black or saturated. It should not be the box-identity or height check.
- Any threshold must be calibrated on this app's own pairs (same pose repeats vs. deliberately changed height vs. different box), with the revision pinned. Report the distribution, not a single number.

### Gaps
- I found no primary source for Revision 2 element count, normalization or distance range. Commonly repeated numbers (for example 2048-D rev1 vs 768-D rev2, unit-norm) could not be verified. Log `elementCount` on device.
- I found no published measurement of feature print sensitivity to scale or viewpoint for near-uniform scenes.
- Whether the default `imageCropAndScaleOption` center-crops (which would change apparent scale sensitivity) was not confirmed from the docs.

## Q3. VNTranslationalImageRegistrationRequest and VNHomographicImageRegistrationRequest: outputs, scale estimation, low-texture behaviour

### Takeaway
The translational request returns only a translation, so it cannot report scale. The homographic request returns a 3×3 perspective warp, so a scale or height change is in principle readable from it. Apple documents neither the algorithm nor a confidence or failure behaviour for low-texture input. Apple's sample says the best alignment occurs when content is "very similar". On a glinting, nearly uniform floor, expect the result to lock onto glints or return an unreliable warp. Treat it as untrusted unless validated.

### Cited Findings
- VNTranslationalImageRegistrationRequest: "determines the affine transform necessary to align the content of two images ... through translation." It returns VNImageTranslationAlignmentObservation with `alignmentTransform` (CGAffineTransform) "to align the floating image with the reference image". iOS 11+. — [VNTranslationalImageRegistrationRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vntranslationalimageregistrationrequest); [VNImageTranslationAlignmentObservation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnimagetranslationalignmentobservation); [alignmentTransform](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnimagetranslationalignmentobservation/alignmenttransform)
- VNHomographicImageRegistrationRequest: "determines the perspective warp matrix necessary to align the content of two images." It returns VNImageHomographicAlignmentObservation with `warpTransform`, "The warp transform matrix to morph the floating image into the reference image". iOS 11+. — [VNHomographicImageRegistrationRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnhomographicimageregistrationrequest); [VNImageHomographicAlignmentObservation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnimagehomographicalignmentobservation); [warpTransform](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnimagehomographicalignmentobservation/warptransform)
- Stateful variants (iOS 17+) register each frame against the previous one: VNTrackTranslationalImageRegistrationRequest and VNTrackHomographicImageRegistrationRequest. These measure frame-to-frame motion, not live-vs-stored-reference. — [VNTrackHomographicImageRegistrationRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vntrackhomographicimageregistrationrequest); [VNTrackTranslationalImageRegistrationRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vntracktranslationalimageregistrationrequest)
- Apple sample "Aligning Similar Images": "The best alignment occurs when the content of the input images is very similar". The sample's input pair is nearly identical, with a slight warp and offset. — [Aligning Similar Images](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/aligning-similar-images)
- Reported failure modes: the reference must exactly match the floating image's resolved ROI pixel dimensions or the request fails. — [objc2-vision docs (mirrors Apple header)](https://docs.rs/objc2-vision/0.3.2/objc2_vision/struct.VNHomographicImageRegistrationRequest.html). A Stack Overflow report describes image-dependent crashes and failures. — [SO 48717723 (mirror)](https://kaweah.freedombox.rocks/kiwix/stackoverflow.com_en_all_2023-11/questions/48717723/why-is-the-vision-framework-unable-to-align-two-images)

### Inferences
- **[own derivation]** For a camera looking straight down at a plane, a height change h→h' appears mainly as isotropic scale s ≈ h/h' in the homography. With warpTransform H normalised so H[2][2]=1, s ≈ sqrt(|det(H upper-left 2×2)|). A 45 cm → 40 cm change is s ≈ 1.125, a 12.5% change, which a working homography would detect easily. The open issue is whether the registration works at all on this scene, not whether the model can express scale.
- Registration (like any area- or feature-based alignment) needs the same texture that NCC needs. There is no documented confidence score, so a sanity test is needed: register reference against reference, and reference against a deliberately shifted or scaled crop of itself, and check that H is recovered. Then repeat on two still-phone frames, which is the real test given glints.
- If the box rim, corners or a marker are in view, the homography has something real to lock onto, and scale estimation becomes plausible.

### Gaps
- Apple does not document the algorithm, the minimum texture needed, or any confidence or quality output for either registration request. I found no benchmark of these requests on low-texture images.
- I could not locate the Swift-native (iOS 18) names or docs for the registration requests (URL guesses 404'd).

## Q4. Other options: phase correlation / Fourier-Mellin, SSIM, histograms, ORB/AKAZE keypoints, edge/structure methods (texture needs, Apple-framework fit)

### Takeaway
Every image-content comparator needs stable structure, whether intensity variance, spectral energy, edges or corners. None of them can recover a pose signal that the scene does not contain. Fourier-Mellin is the one classical method that estimates scale directly and can be built on Accelerate. SSIM's stabilising constants hide the zero-variance problem rather than solve it. Histograms are blind to geometry. Keypoint matchers (ORB/AKAZE) are third-party and fail on textureless scenes.

### Cited Findings
- **Phase correlation / Fourier-Mellin.** Reddy & Chatterji (IEEE TIP 5(8):1266–1271, 1996) extend phase correlation to translation, rotation and scale. They use Fourier scaling and rotation properties: translation-invariant magnitude spectrum, then log-polar resampling, then phase correlation gives rotation and log-scale. The abstract reports good robustness to random noise. — [Reddy & Chatterji 1996 (dblp)](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/tip/ReddyC96.html); [scilit record](https://scilit.com/publications/84516cb04acd45844169bca7eb81ec35)
- Fourier-Mellin and cross-correlation lose robustness when the usable structured content is a tiny fraction of the image (example: ~1%). — [US 8390291 patent](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8390291)
- Log-polar sampling limits angular and scale resolution (community answer on <1° rotations). — [MathWorks Answers 88985](https://in.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/answers/88985). Log-magnitude spectra reduce low-frequency dominance and give more stable log-polar estimates. — [arXiv 2608.11565](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11565)
- Apple fit: Accelerate provides 2D FFTs (`vDSP.FFT2D`, iOS 13+) and DFT routines. — [vDSP.FFT2D](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accelerate/vdsp/fft2d); [Performing Fourier transforms on interleaved-complex data](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/accelerate/performing-fourier-transforms-on-interleaved-complex-data). Log-polar resampling has no built-in API; it must be hand-coded with vDSP/vImage, a custom Core Image kernel or Metal (all Apple frameworks).
- **SSIM.** C1 = (K1·L)² and C2 = (K2·L)², with K1 = 0.01 and K2 = 0.03, were added "to avoid instability when the denominators ... were too small", that is, on nearly flat windows. — [Seshadrinathan & Bovik, ICIP 2008](https://www.live.ece.utexas.edu/publications/2008/ks_unifying_icip08.pdf); [Understanding SSIM (arXiv 2006.13846)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.13846). SSIM's structure term is the local NCC, so it inherits Q1. On flat windows the constants push SSIM toward 1, so two different flat floors score as "similar". No built-in Apple SSIM; computable with vImage/Accelerate box filters.
- **Keypoints.** Shi-Tomasi and Harris-type detectors need windows with two large structure-tensor eigenvalues (Q1 source). A uniform floor yields none, and glints yield false, unstable ones. ORB, AKAZE, SIFT and ArUco live in OpenCV **[3rd-party]**, not Apple frameworks. A 2026 matching paper notes that many registration methods fail on textureless imagery. — [HOME: Hough-space matching for structured and textureless videos, arXiv 2607.25389](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25389)
- Apple's own image-detection guidance for ARKit says "Images with high contrast work best", and that glossy or reflective surfaces "can interfere with detection". This is a first-party statement that Apple's tracking needs contrast and is hurt by reflections. — [Detecting Images in an AR Experience](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/detecting-images-in-an-ar-experience)

### Inferences
- **Histograms and mean brightness:** they need no texture but carry no geometry. They can catch exposure, torch and "wrong scene" changes. They won't catch a height change on a uniform floor (same distribution, slightly different vignetting). They'll catch a different box only if its tint or brightness differs.
- **4×4 brightness map:** on a torch-lit floor it mostly encodes the torch hotspot and vignetting falloff. At a fixed camera-torch geometry, the hotspot's apparent size and falloff do shift with height (inverse-square plus spot footprint). This is a weak but real height cue that needs no texture. It is also sensitive to water depth, torch level and auto-exposure, so lock exposure and ISO via AVFoundation if relying on it. **[own inference, not sourced]**
- **Edge or structure comparison** (for example Sobel or Canny via vImage or Core Image `CIEdges`, then a distance transform or chamfer): this works only if real edges (rim, corners, marker) are in the frame. At the current framing the edges are out of view, so it collapses to glint edges.
- **Ranking for this scene:** marker or edges in view > Fourier-Mellin/homography on a frame that includes structure > feature print / histogram as coarse sanity > NCC on uniform floor (unreliable).

### Gaps
- I found no study that quantitatively compares phase correlation, Fourier-Mellin, SSIM and keypoints specifically on near-uniform, specular water scenes.
- The SSIM rationale is from Bovik-group and secondary sources. I did not retrieve the 2004 Wang et al. TIP paper text itself.

## Q5. What practitioners do when the scene is texture-poor (fiducials, wider FOV, different signal)

### Takeaway
The proven fix is to add known structure rather than to find a cleverer comparator. Put a fiducial (printed code or pattern) where the camera sees it, or widen the framing so the box rim and corners are in view. Apple-native routes exist: Vision barcode/QR detection gives four corners plus a payload, and ARKit reference images give full pose from a known physical size. Together they directly solve both (a) box identity and (b) height/scale.

### Cited Findings
- Fiducials are the standard answer for camera pose in "cluttered or textureless environments". Calvet et al. use a planar pattern of circular dots for reliable detection and identification across views. — [Calvet et al., CVPR 2016](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2016/papers/Calvet_Detection_and_Accurate_CVPR_2016_paper.pdf). Other designs include printed square binary markers ([DodecaPen, UIST 2017](https://research.facebook.com/file/565262174921432/uist2017_pen.pdf)), lenticular markers that are less noise-sensitive than a 4-point pattern ([GWU](https://www2.seas.gwu.edu/~pless/papers/3DVlenticular.pdf)), and printable surface-like codes for 6-DoF tracking ([Ninja Codes, arXiv 2510.18976](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.18976)).
- **Apple-native fiducial: Vision barcode detection.** `VNDetectBarcodesRequest` detects barcodes and returns `VNBarcodeObservation`. That observation inherits from `VNRectangleObservation`, so it carries the four corner points, plus `payloadStringValue`/`payloadData` and `symbology`. Supported symbologies include QR, microQR, Aztec, DataMatrix and PDF417. Swift-native `DetectBarcodesRequest` is iOS 18. — [VNDetectBarcodesRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vndetectbarcodesrequest); [VNBarcodeObservation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnbarcodeobservation); [VNBarcodeSymbology](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnbarcodesymbology); [DetectBarcodesRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/detectbarcodesrequest)
- **Apple-native fiducial: ARKit reference images.** ARKit needs "knowledge of the image's real-world dimensions" and uses the entered physical size "to determine the distance of the image from the camera". Images must be flat, high contrast and not glossy, since reflections interfere. The anchor's transform gives position and orientation. — [ARReferenceImage](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arreferenceimage); [Detecting Images in an AR Experience](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/detecting-images-in-an-ar-experience)
- Feature- and correlation-based methods fail where there are too few features (air, underwater scenes), which motivates adding structure or changing signal. — [CS270 report](https://kuanghaofei.github.io/pdf/cs270_report.pdf) (student report, low authority)

### Inferences
- **Recommended design (Apple-only), and why it answers (a) and (b):**
  1. Fix a small matte QR, DataMatrix or Aztec label to the box rim (above the water line, to avoid glints and refraction) or under the floor at a known spot. Encode a box ID in it.
  2. (a) A different box shows as a payload mismatch: a discrete, noise-free check that no image-similarity score can match.
  3. (b) Camera height and scale come from the marker's apparent side length in pixels compared with the reference (pixel size ∝ 1/distance for a fixed lens at 1x). Tilt comes from corner-quad distortion, with CoreMotion gravity as a cross-check. For a 45 → 40 cm change the side length grows about 12.5%, far above corner-localisation jitter for a marker spanning tens of pixels. **[own derivation]**
  4. Optionally use two markers at opposite rim corners to also check lateral position and rotation.
- **Wider FOV:** if the rim and corners enter the frame, VNDetectRectanglesRequest, edges or the homographic request get real structure. This trades off against counting resolution on the larvae, so the 0.5x ultra-wide could serve as a separate "pose check" capture. **[own inference]**
- **Different signal entirely:** CoreMotion gives tilt and attitude, but not height. Measuring height without texture needs a depth signal (ARKit sceneDepth / AVFoundation depth on LiDAR or dual-camera devices), or a physical jig or stand that fixes height mechanically. The physical jig is the most reliable and needs no software gate. **[own inference]**
- Practical gate structure in line with "few, tunable gates": (1) marker found, payload matches the box ID, (2) marker scale ratio within ±x%, (3) marker centre offset within ±y px, (4) exposure/brightness sanity. Drop NCC on the bare floor, or keep it only as a logged diagnostic.

### Gaps
- I found no source measuring how well water depth over a submerged printed marker affects Vision barcode detection (refraction plus glints). A rim placement above water avoids the question. Test it empirically.
- I found no source on LiDAR/ToF depth reliability over shallow water on white styrofoam. IR behaviour on water is plausibly problematic but unverified here.
- I found no published benchmark of Vision barcode corner-point accuracy (pixel jitter). Measure on device to set the scale-ratio tolerance.
